Section: The system, not the model, is the useful unit of analysis
Claim: A language model becomes operationally general only when wrapped in memory, planning loops, tools, permissions, and feedback; capability and deployment should be evaluated separately.
Source: User's thesis; Morris et al., Levels of AGI (arXiv:2311.02462).

Section: The LLM is classical linear algebra at industrial scale
Claim: Transformer inference is ordinary numerical computation - matrix multiplications, normalization, nonlinearities, and repeated attention - not quantum physics.
Source: Vaswani et al., Attention Is All You Need (arXiv:1706.03762).

Section: High-dimensional space is strange without being mystical
Claim: A hidden state is a vector in R^d; meaning is distributed across many coordinates and directions, making single-neuron stories unreliable.
Source: Transformer architecture; explanatory synthesis.

Section: We can record everything and still not understand it
Claim: Opacity comes from scale, distributed representation, context sensitivity, nonlinear composition, and incomplete causal tools - not from information moving too quickly to observe.
Source: Anthropic, Tracing the thoughts of a large language model (2025).

Section: Intelligence does not settle consciousness
Claim: Task competence, agency, and subjective experience are different questions; current behavior does not establish consciousness, and lack of consciousness would not eliminate practical risk.
Source: Colombatto et al., Neuroscience of Consciousness (2024); Butlin et al. as discussed there.

Section: Physical access converts inference into consequence
Claim: The risk escalates when a capable model gains durable goals, broad permissions, persistence, feedback, and actuators; a robot is one actuator among many.
Source: User's thesis; Morris et al. on autonomy and risk.

Section: Govern the whole stack
Claim: Safety controls should be placed at tools, permissions, budgets, verification, and physical interlocks rather than relying only on the model's intentions or explanations.
Source: Synthesis of cited work and standard systems engineering principles.
